The rules `STYLE.md` does not fix, for The Seifert--van Kampen theorem, after TauCeti: the library
TauCeti at commit `37f5e9dce5ac83d2825198ac72d7f5d64eb26c85`, and the theorem
`TauCeti.isPushout_fundamentalGroup`. Every drafter, attacker, judge and editor reads this file beside `STYLE.md`.
The local introduction rules below supersede that file's requirement to put all
ambient variables and notion markers into a separate conventions section.

## Local introductions

Introduce spaces, subsets, basepoints and families in the section that uses them.
Introduce specialized objects, such as the ordered group for the subdivision
formula or the chosen connecting morphisms, beside the result that needs them.
Use direct mathematical sentences: "Let S be a subset of a topological space X."
Do not reserve a catalogue of letters for the whole text.

Keep a letter's meaning fixed within an argument. A later section may use the
same letter for a different object when it introduces that object explicitly.
State every extra assumption where it is used: the pseudometric assumption in
Lemma 1.1, for example, is local to that lemma. Use a separate space $Z$ and
compact subset $K$ there. Introduce the general topological space $X$ only
when the path results begin; do not impose a pseudometric or vector-space
structure on it.

## Assumed background

Assume basic familiarity with point-set topology. Do not explain neighborhoods,
interiors, compactness, subspace topologies, set membership or the range of a map.
Introduce notation when it is needed, and keep conventions that affect the
mathematics, such as the nonemptiness of path-connected spaces and subsets.
Do not retain introductory prose solely to create a notion annotation.
Treat paths in a subspace as familiar: introduce $\gamma^S$ only as notation,
without a numbered definition or an explanation of its pointwise construction.

## Standing hypotheses

One row per key of `hypotheses.json`: the macro, the words it prints, and the Lean binder
it stands for.

| macro | prints | stands for |
|---|---|---|
| `\hypCoverTwo` | the interiors of `A` and `B` cover `X` | the printed type `interior A ∪ interior B = Set.univ` |
| `\hypCoverFamily` | each point of `X` has some `U_i` as a neighborhood | a printed type `∀ (y : X), ∃ i, U i ∈ nhds y`, for any one name `y` |
| `\hypPathConnected` | `A`, `B` and `A ∩ B` are path connected | the printed types `IsPathConnected A`, `IsPathConnected B` and `IsPathConnected (A ∩ B)`, which always appear together |
| `\hypBasepoint` | `x` lies in `A` and in `B` | the printed types `x ∈ A` and `x ∈ B` |

Write the macro, never the words by hand, and write it only where the key declaration of
the item or part binds the hypothesis. The pairwise condition of the indexed statements,
`∀ (i j : I), IsPathConnected (U i ∩ U j)`, and the indexed basepoint condition,
`∀ (i : I), x ∈ U i`, are not among these four: state them in words. The Lebesgue number
lemma's neighborhood condition, `∀ x ∈ s, ∃ i, c i ∈ nhds x`, covers a subset rather than
the whole space, so it too is stated in words.

## Numbers, coercions and junk values

`\N` starts at `0`. A parameter is a point of `\uI`; it is compared with and combined with
real numbers through its value in `\R`, so an expression such as `2u - 1` is read in `\R`
and no subtraction is taken in `\N`. The passage from a point of a subset `S` to the point
of `X` under it, and back along `\codres{\gamma}{S}`, is left silent except where a
statement is about it; so is the reading of a basepoint `x` of `X` lying in `S` as the
basepoint of `S` in `\fgrp{S}{x}`. The points of a subdivision are nondecreasing, not
strictly increasing.

Definition 1.2 defines subdivision points directly by `t_n = min{b, a + nd}`
for a nonnegative step size. Do not restate this formula as a separate lemma.
Its annotation links to the Lean lemma identifying Mathlib's interval-valued
construction with that formula. The unused negative-step case, where Mathlib
also clips at the left endpoint, need not appear in the text.

The subpath `\subpath{\gamma}{r}{s}` is formed for every pair of parameters,
running backwards when `s` is smaller than `r` and constant when they agree.
`items.json` records no `junk` entry for any key declaration, so no item needs
a junk sentence of its own.

## The reference work

The text cites A. Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Cambridge University Press, 2002 by number only, through `\fcref`: never a sentence of the work, and never a close paraphrase. `\fcref{<number>}` is for a number the library's docstrings give, `\fcref*{<number>}`, with a dagger, for one inferred from the work's order (`items.json`, each `reference.source`).

Every number this text cites comes from a module docstring, so every `\fcref` is unstarred
and no dagger is printed anywhere. `items.json` gives `Theorem 1.20` for the four items
that carry a reference, two of them in section 4 and one each in sections 5 and 6, and no
number anywhere else. The four are items `4.1` and `4.2`, item `5.1` and item `6.4`; a
drafter of section 4 writes two `\fcref`, one on each of those two items. Every one of the
four numbers belongs to the item as a whole, so no part of an item carries a number of its
own.

## Proofs and constructions

The maintainer requested actual proofs, allowing concise proof sketches, on
2026-11-06. This overrides the statements-only and no-proof-sketch rules of
STYLE.md. A selected proof must give its construction, the equalities or
closure arguments it needs, and the deduction of the stated conclusion.
Use earlier numbered results explicitly; do not replace an argument with a
statement of its intended strategy. Additional notation is local to the proof
where it is introduced. The `proofpassage` environment only formats the argument. Mark selected
sentences with `\proofstep` and individual displayed equations with
`\proofequation`; each must name exactly one matching Lean range in
`proofsteps.json`. Never mark a whole proof as one region. Lean names remain
in the annotations.

In the proof of 5.1, use $H$ for the generated subgroup and $S$ for the
subgroupoid defined by transporting morphisms to the basepoint, matching
the local names in Lean. Here $S=\top$ means that this subgroupoid is the
whole fundamental groupoid. This use of $S$ is local to the proof; section 1 introduces its own subset $S$.

Prefer mathematical notation that makes a linked argument easy to recognize:
use the chosen index $i_0$ in 5.1, and introduce the equal-subdivision value
$L_N$ before its common value $L$ in 4.2. There $N$ counts the pieces, $N_0$ is one fixed fine choice, and $m$ is
a positive integer refining each piece. Introduce intermediate objects by their mathematical meaning, and
separate deductions when they have different proof-step links. Keep tactic
syntax, helper names, coercions and other Lean bookkeeping out of the printed
proofs. Preserve familiar mathematical notation where it reads more naturally.
