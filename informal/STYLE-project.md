TauCeti at commit `37f5e9dce5ac83d2825198ac72d7f5d64eb26c85`, and the theorem
`TauCeti.isPushout_fundamentalGroup`. Every drafter, attacker, judge and editor reads this file beside `STYLE.md`.
The text follows the shared editorial guide, with the local choices below.

## Local introductions

Introduce spaces, subsets, basepoints and families in the section that uses them.
Introduce specialized objects, such as the chosen connecting morphisms, beside
the result that needs them.
Use direct mathematical sentences: "Let S be a subset of a topological space X."
Do not reserve a catalogue of letters for the whole text.

Keep a letter's meaning fixed within an argument. A later section may use the
same letter for a different object when it introduces that object explicitly.
State every extra assumption where it is used. In Lemma 1.3, use a compact
subset $K$ of Euclidean space; this assumption is local to the lemma. Introduce
the general topological space $X$ only when the path results begin; do not
impose a metric or vector-space structure on it.

## Editorial focus

Communicate the constructions, key lemmas and deductions that explain why the
Seifert--van Kampen theorem holds. The dependency closure is a source inventory,
not a requirement to give every Lean declaration a numbered statement.
Leave routine bookkeeping implicit: pointwise concatenation formulas,
restriction followed by inclusion, subpath containment and endpoint transports
need no standalone lemmas. Retain short notation introductions where needed.
Keep the subdivision and homotopy-class reassembly results because they carry
the passage from local paths to the global argument. Omitted implementation
helpers may remain in the item table without creating PDF statements or links.
In generation statements and arguments, say that the fundamental groupoids
of the cover members generate the ambient groupoid, leaving their inclusion
images implicit after one short convention in Section 2. Retain "subgroupoid"
for the groupoid argument. Never globally identify a subspace's fundamental
groupoid with its image: inclusion can identify distinct path classes, and
the gluing functors must have the actual subspace groupoids as their domains.
Introduce fundamental groupoids and gluing in Section 2, fundamental-group
generation in Section 3, and the constructions relating groupoids to their vertex
groups beside their use in Section 4. A section must advance the argument, rather
than collect notation used much later. Put one-use variables in their result;
combine repeated setup into a connected paragraph. Remove special-case announcements,
background definitions and repeated verification of earlier constructions.

## Assumed background

Assume familiarity with categories, groupoids and monoid homomorphisms; do not
add standalone reminders of their defining properties.
Assume basic familiarity with point-set topology. Do not explain neighborhoods,
interiors, compactness, subspace topologies, set membership or the range of a map.
Introduce notation when it is needed, and keep conventions that affect the
mathematics, such as the nonemptiness of path-connected subsets.
Do not retain introductory prose solely to create a notion annotation.
Treat paths in a subspace as familiar. Do not introduce separate notation for
regarding a path as a path in a subspace containing its image. In the generation
proof, say directly that each piece's class lies in the image of the
inclusion-induced fundamental-groupoid functor.
Define subpath parametrization by its affine parameter formula. Describe the
action of the induced fundamental-groupoid functor on points and path classes.
Check adjacent section openings for duplicate introductions of inclusion notation.

## Standing hypotheses

One row per key of `hypotheses.json`: the macro, the words it prints, and the Lean binder
it stands for.

| macro | prints | stands for |
|---|---|---|
| `\hypCoverTwo` | the interiors of `A` and `B` cover `X` | the printed type `interior A ∪ interior B = Set.univ` |
| `\hypCoverFamily` | for each `y ∈ X`, some `U_i` is a neighborhood of `y` | a printed type `∀ (y : X), ∃ i, U i ∈ nhds y`, for any one name `y` |
| `\hypPathConnected` | `A`, `B` and `A ∩ B` are path connected | the printed types `IsPathConnected A`, `IsPathConnected B` and `IsPathConnected (A ∩ B)`, which always appear together |
| `\hypBasepoint` | `x` lies in `A` and in `B` | the printed types `x ∈ A` and `x ∈ B` |

Write the macro, never the words by hand. In Sections 2 and 3, state
`\hypCoverFamily` once in the opening prose of an `fcassumptions` block;
following items in that block inherit the assumption. In Section 2, introduce
the fundamental groupoid and induced functors before this cover assumption.
Use $y$ for its quantified point to distinguish it from the basepoint $x$ in
Section 3. End each block before unrelated results. Keep the quantifier order: each point has
some cover member as a neighborhood, and the member may depend on the point.
Other hypothesis macros stay in the item or part whose key declaration binds
them. The pairwise condition of the indexed statements,
`∀ (i j : I), IsPathConnected (U i ∩ U j)`, and the indexed basepoint condition,
`∀ (i : I), x ∈ U i`, are not among these four: state them in words. The Lebesgue number
lemma's neighborhood condition, `∀ x ∈ s, ∃ i, c i ∈ nhds x`, covers a subset rather than
the whole space, so it too is stated in words.

## Where notation is introduced

There is no separate conventions section. Give each section a short local setup,
and explain specialized notation next to its first use:

- Section 1: path notation, subdivision and subpath composition, followed by the
  compactness lemma used for uniform subdivisions in the gluing construction.
- Section 2: fundamental groupoids, inclusion functors, generation and gluing.
- Section 3: fundamental groups and generation by subspaces.
- Section 4: the two vertex-group functors, then descent and the pushout proof.

Repeat enough ambient data and notation to make a section readable on its own.
Keep typeclass syntax, universe variables and other Lean bookkeeping out of the
printed text. Mathematical hypotheses remain those of the declarations, except
for an explicitly recorded specialization. Lemma 1.3 specializes the linked
pseudometric theorem to Euclidean space with compact subset $K$. The family
members, neighborhoods and balls are ambient in Euclidean space, just as in
the Lean theorem; the conclusion needs no intersection with $K$. Its link is
`broadMatch`, because the Lean theorem is more general. For the interval and
square applications, choose ambient open sets whose intersections with the
parameter domain lie in the relevant preimages. With the
Euclidean metric, choose the homotopy grid by cell diameter, not side length;
the Lean proof instead uses the equivalent maximum metric.

## Numbers, coercions and junk values

`\N` starts at `0`. A parameter is a point of `\uI`; it is compared with and combined with
real numbers through its value in `\R`, so an expression such as `2u - 1` is read in `\R`
and no subtraction is taken in `\N`. The passage from a point of a subset `S` to the point
of `X` under it, and regarding a path whose image lies in `S` as a path in `S`,
are left implicit except where a statement is about them; so is the reading of
a basepoint `x` of `X` lying in `S` as the
basepoint of `S` in `\fgrp{S}{x}`. The points of a subdivision are nondecreasing, not
strictly increasing.

Use the subdivision-existence proposition directly in the exposition. Do not
introduce the general clipped-step construction, its ordered-group setup or
its numerical example. These are implementation details of the Lean library.
When the gluing proof needs equal subdivisions, introduce $t_k=k/N$ there.
In the proof of Proposition 1.1, choose a finite subdivision with interval
lengths at most half the Lebesgue radius, matching the Mathlib helper used by
TauCeti. Link the mesh choice and interval-containment argument separately
to that helper. Keep its clipped-step implementation implicit; do not claim
that it chooses reciprocal-integer step sizes.

The subpath `\subpath{\gamma}{r}{s}` is formed for every pair of parameters,
running backwards when `s` is smaller than `r` and constant when they agree.
`items.json` records no `junk` entry for any key declaration, so no item needs
a junk sentence of its own.

## Proofs and constructions

The maintainer requested actual proofs, allowing concise proof sketches, on
2026-11-06. This specializes the shared proof-writing rules. A selected proof must give its construction, the equalities or
closure arguments it needs, and the deduction of the stated conclusion.
Use earlier numbered results explicitly; do not replace an argument with a
statement of its intended strategy. Additional notation is local to the proof
where it is introduced. The `proofpassage` environment only formats the argument. Mark selected
sentences with `\proofstep` and individual displayed equations with
`\proofequation`; each must name exactly one matching Lean range in
`proofsteps.json`. Never mark a whole proof as one region. Lean names remain
in the annotations.

In the proof of 3.1, use $H$ for the generated subgroup and $S$ for the
subgroupoid defined by transporting morphisms to the basepoint, matching
the local names in Lean. Here $S=\top$ means that this subgroupoid is the
whole fundamental groupoid. This use of $S$ is local to the proof; section 1 introduces its own subset $S$.

Prefer mathematical notation that makes a linked argument easy to recognize:
use the chosen index $i_0$ in 3.1, and introduce the equal-subdivision value
$L_N$ before its common value $L$ in 2.2. There $N$ counts the pieces, $N_0$ is another fine choice, and $m$ is
a positive integer refining each piece. Introduce intermediate objects by their mathematical meaning, and
separate deductions when they have different proof-step links. Keep tactic
syntax, helper names, coercions and other Lean bookkeeping out of the printed
proofs. Preserve familiar mathematical notation where it reads more naturally.

For the homotopy-invariance argument in 2.2, write the horizontal paths as

adjacent rows, then from the first row to the last. Keep the cell-containment
