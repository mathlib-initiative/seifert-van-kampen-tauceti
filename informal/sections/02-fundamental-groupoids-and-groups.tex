Let $X$ be a topological space.  Its fundamental groupoid
\fcnotion{FundamentalGroupoid}{$\fgpd{X}$} has the points of $X$ as objects
and homotopy classes of paths as morphisms, composed by concatenation.
Here a \fcnotion{PathConnectedSpace}{path-connected space} is understood to
be nonempty.

\begin{lemma}[id=2.1]{The fundamental groupoid of a path-connected space}
Suppose $X$ is path connected, and let $x,y\in X$.  Then there is a morphism
of $\fgpd{X}$ from $x$ to $y$.
\leanref{FundamentalGroupoid.nonempty_hom}
\end{lemma}

Let $f:X\longrightarrow Y$ be a continuous map between topological spaces.
The induced \fcnotion{CategoryTheory.Functor}{functor}
\fcnotion{FundamentalGroupoid.map}{$\fgpd{f}$} sends points to their images
and path classes to the classes of their images.  For a path $\gamma$,
\fcnotion{Path.map}{$f\circ\gamma$} is its image path in $Y$.

\begin{lemma}[id=2.2]{An injective map is injective on objects}
Suppose $f$ is injective.  Then $\fgpd{f}$ is injective on objects.
\leanref{ContinuousMap.fundamentalGroupoid_map_obj_injective}
\end{lemma}

A \fcnotion{CategoryTheory.Subgroupoid}{subgroupoid} of $\fgpd{X}$ specifies
sets of morphisms between pairs of points, closed under composition and
inverses.  Equalities of endpoints also
\fcnotion{Path.Homotopic.Quotient.cast}{rename the endpoints of a path class}.

\begin{lemma}[id=2.3]{Recasting the endpoints of a path class}
Let $\mathcal H$ be a subgroupoid of $\fgpd{X}$, let $\omega$ be a class of
paths from $x$ to $y$, and let $x',y'\in X$ satisfy $x'=x$ and $y'=y$.
Then $\omega$ with its endpoints renamed to $x'$ and $y'$ lies in
$\mathcal H$ if and only if $\omega$ does.
\leanref{Path.Homotopic.Quotient.cast_mem_arrows_iff}
\end{lemma}

At a basepoint $x\in X$, the \fcnotion{FundamentalGroup}{fundamental group}
$\fgrp{X}{x}$ is the group of morphisms from $x$ to itself in $\fgpd{X}$.
Multiplication is composition, with the right factor traversed first.
The \fcnotion{FundamentalGroup.fromPath}{class of a loop at $x$} is an element
of this group.  The map $f$ induces a homomorphism
\fcnotion{FundamentalGroup.map}{$\fgrp{f}{x}$} from $\fgrp{X}{x}$ to
$\fgrp{Y}{f(x)}$.

\begin{lemma}[id=2.4]{The image of the class of a loop}
Let $x\in X$ and let $\gamma$ be a loop at $x$.  Then $\fgrp{f}{x}$ sends
$\pcls{\gamma}$ to $\pcls{f\circ\gamma}$.
\leanref{TauCeti.FundamentalGroup.map_fromPath}
\end{lemma}
